# Report 9 — Reducing the number of sprites in the Evolve pixel-art view

Date: 2026-10-13. This report follows report 8 (`8-sprites-evolve-ere1.md`, 541 images for era 1). Sources read in `src/`: `actions.js`, `tech.js`, `jobs.js`, `civics.js`, `vars.js`, `seasons.js`, `races.js`, `debug.js` and `main.js`. I executed nothing in Evolve and modified nothing. A throwaway prototype is in `work/proto-sprites/` (§ 6.6).

## In brief

- **Before**: 541 whole images to draw, i.e. about **350,000 pixels** placed by hand (estimate in § 1).
- **After**: **136 small hand-drawn pieces** (8×8 textures, tools, hats, accessories, a few silhouettes), i.e. about **27,000 pixels**. The code composes the rest:
  - 78 base buildings (26 types × 3 tiers), with a practically unlimited number of variants;
  - 17 jobs × 7 frames × 2 directions, then the mirror;
  - the terrain, water, weather, effects, moon and construction site.
- **Gain**: about **÷4 in number of drawings** and **÷13 in drawn area**. In exchange, the share of code increases: a generator of about 1,500 lines of JavaScript, with no dependency.
- **Three big savings**:
  1. **buildings as isometric boxes generated by the code**, dressed with 9 8×8 textures, modules and 18 "signatures";
  2. **characters in layers**: 2 bodies, 5 shared arm poses, and it is the tool that makes the job;
  3. **everything that is particle, ground, water, sky and weather is drawn by the code**, with no image at all (about 240 images of report 8 disappear).

(Areas: variants × animation frames × width × height, using the sizes of report 8. For categories without a stated size, I took 16×16 or 32×16. This is an order of magnitude.)

**Three root causes**:
1. **We draw products instead of factors.** A "walking farmer" is the product (body × outfit × tool × pose). If we draw the factors, the code does the multiplications.
2. **We draw things that the code traces better.** Ground diamonds, water, smoke, rain, sundial, moon, progress bars, scaffolding: these are geometric primitives or particles.
3. **We animate by redrawing the whole object.** A windmill of which only the sails turn needs only an animated "sails" layer.

## 2. Buildings: tiers, modules, families, placement

### 2.1 Principle: the generated isometric box

A building = **base + box(es) + roof + modules + signature + accessories**, stacked by the code.

- **Box**: the code projects a parallelepiped of given size (width, depth, height in px) in 2:1. It fills the left face (lit), the right face (shaded) and the top, by sampling an **8×8 texture** in the face's frame. No per-building drawing.
- **Roof**: shapes generated by the code (gable, hip, flat with rim, cone, dome), filled with a roofing texture.
- **Base and foundation**: a 2 to 3 px band of dry stone, generated by the code.
- **Modules**: small drawn pieces (door, window, column, flag, barrel), glued to **anchor points** computed on the faces.
- **Signature**: the hand-drawn piece that says what the building is (mill sails, mine headframe, anvil, bell...). It carries the identity.

**Major benefit for lighting.** Report 8 obtains the 2nd direction by mirroring. But in isometric, mirroring a building puts the lit face on the right and breaks the light, which comes from the left. A generated box is **recomputed** in the other direction with the right light. Mirroring remains used only for small pieces, where the error is not visible.

**Generic modules (11)**: plain door, arched door, shuttered window, glazed window, column, flag, barrel, ladder, and 3 variety accessories (flower pot, hanging laundry, leaning tool). Doors and windows are **drawn already slanted for the left face**. The right face = the mirror, one tone darker. ### 2.3 The 3 evolution tiers (read from `global.tech`)

I propose **two evolution axes, plus one variation per instance**:

**(a) Material tier, common to the whole village and driven by technology.** The whole village changes era at once, which reads well and rewards a research.

| Tier | Trigger (keys verified in `src/tech.js`) | Walls | Roof | Details |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| P1 "Primitive" | start (`tech.primitive`) | logs, wattle-and-daub | thatch, cone or low gable | no foundation, hides, logs |
| P2 "Timber frame" | `tech.axe >= 2` (copper_axes) | planks, dry-stone lower part | shingles | shuttered windows, chimney, base |
| P3 "Masonry" | `tech.cement >= 1` (cement) | cut stone, brick | tiles | glazed windows, upper floor, stoop |

For industry and extraction, the metal follows `tech.mining` (2 = metal_working, 3 = iron_mining) and `tech.smelting` (1, then 2 = steel). Tool heads, the mine cart and the hoops move from the copper ramp to the iron ramp, then to the steel ramp. It is a simple recoloring.

**(b) Density, specific to each type and driven by the counter.** When there are more buildings than visible sprites (§ 2.5), each sprite represents several buildings and grows by composition:
- housing: lean-to, then upper floor;
- farm: 2 more fields;
- storage: one more pile;
- industry: a 2nd chimney and more smoke.

**(c) Variation per instance, drawn from a seed (type, rank).** Each instance draws:

| Parameter | Possible choices |
|---|---|
| Roof ramp | 3 to choose from, compatible with the tier |
| Wall shade | 2 |
| Door | 2 models × left or right face |
| Windows | 0 to 2 |
| Chimney | present or not, and its position |
| Wall height | ±2 px |
| Roof | gable or hip |
| Orientation | reversed or not, the box being regenerated |
| Accessories | 0 to 2 among 6 |

That gives more than 2,000 combinations for a cabin, of which about thirty are really distinguishable at first glance. **Anti-clone rule**: two neighboring slots must differ on at least 2 criteria among (roof, wall shade, orientation, accessory). If not, redraw with the seed + 1.

**What this gives**: 26 types × 3 tiers = **78 generated base buildings**, each with dozens of variants. Report 8 had only 33, with no evolution at all.

The "animated buildings" of report 8 (11 × 4 frames) become a fixed building, plus an animated layer (sails, blade, mine cart) or particles (smoke, fire, dust). Animation then costs **0 more building images**.

We can also use the `on` field that some buildings have in the game (§ 8): a stopped building has neither smoke nor turning sails.

### 2.5 Automatic placement from a counter

Evolve only provides `global.city.<key>.count`, with no map. I propose this rule:

1. **Fixed map** of about 24 × 24 tiles around a central square: the campfire, then the village square.
2. **One sector per family**, so that the village looks organized:
   - housing and society around the square;
   - food in an outer ring (the fields);
   - extraction at the edge, on the side of the hills and the forest;
   - industry downwind (its smoke leaves the village).
3. **Pre-computed slots** in each sector, in a spiral, skipping the paths. **Instance no. k of a type always takes slot no. k, with the seed (type, k).** Thus, building the 13th farm does not move the other 12: the player finds their village again.
4. **Number of visible sprites** for a counter n:
   `v(n) = n` if n ≤ 8, otherwise `v(n) = min(cap, 8 + round(4 × log2(n / 8)))`.
   - Examples: 16 → 12, 32 → 16, 64 → 20, 128 → 24.
   - Cap per type: 24. Global cap: about 80 visible buildings.
5. **Density** `d = n / v(n)`:
   - d < 1.5: normal sprite;
   - d between 1.5 and 3: annex;
   - d ≥ 3: annex and upper floor, with more smoke and more workers around.
6. **Construction site**: when a counter increases, the new slot shows 2 to 3 seconds of scaffolding drawn by the code (the box edges as wooden lines), with dust as particles.

### 3.3 Minimal animations

| Animation | Frames displayed | Drawings needed |
|---|---|---|
| Idle | 1, plus a 1 px breathing done by the code (2 frames are seen) | shared |
| Walk | 4: step A, passing, step B, passing | legs: 4 poses; the dress: 2 hems; swinging arms = neutral pose; 1 px bounce by the code |
| "Strike" work (7 jobs: lumberjack, stonecutter, 2 miners, farmer, craftsman, cement worker) | 2: tool raised, tool brought down | 2 arm poses. **The tool is the same drawing rotated by 90° by the code**: a 90° rotation is exact in pixel art, unlike 45°. |

**Directions**:
- the **mirror** gives left and right (there is no lighting problem at 16 px);
- the **back** is only useful for walking away. I propose 8 back pieces: 2 torsos, 1 swinging arm, 1 head, 4 hair cuts. The front legs and headgear are reused, with 1 px offset; at this size, the difference is not visible.

**Scale**: do not shrink by a non-integer factor (pixel art blurs). To make children, one more body would be needed; this is not useful in era 1.

**Number of visible citizens**: `w(n) = min(n, 1 + ceil(sqrt(n)))` per job, with a global cap of 40. For example, 1 worker → 1 visible citizen, 9 → 4, 100 → 11.

### 3.5 What is lost in readability, and how to compensate

**What is lost**: at 16×24, a forager and a farmer only differ by 3 to 5 px of tool and hat. The job is no longer read "from the face". Each character is also less expressive than if hand-drawn.

**Compensations, in order of effectiveness**:
1. **The place**: a citizen works in front of their building. A man with a pickaxe in front of the gallery is a miner.
2. **The outfit color per family**, constant, and reused in the interface. It reads before the shape.
3. **Exaggerated tools**: 2 px thick, a light and contrasting tool head that sticks out of the silhouette.
4. **The gesture**: strike or gesture in front. Two movement families are distinguishable from afar.
5. **A floating icon** on touch, or above a group: the already-drawn 16×16 resource icon (the job produces this resource), with the number of workers. It costs no more drawings.

**How far it stays beautiful, honestly**:
- **Buildings**: the generated boxes are clean and consistent in lighting, but **more "geometric"** than hand-drawn buildings. The edges are too straight and there is no wear. Three countermeasures:
  1. a 1 px noise on the roof and base edges;
  2. carefully made signatures and accessories;
  3. a **hand touch-up of the 5 or 6 most-seen buildings** (cabin, cottage, farm, lumber yard, quarry), on top of the generated rendering, as detail pieces.

  Count **+10 to 15 pieces** if you want this level of finish. It is the only serious reservation.
- **Characters**: at 16×24, layered composition is **the norm of the genre** and is not visible. The limit is expressiveness, and the countermeasure is already described in § 3.5.
- **Particles and terrain**: they are livelier than loops of 4 to 6 frames. The risk is "too clean": a perfectly uniform ground. Details must be sown, for example tufts and pebbles at 10%.
- **Reasonable floor**: below about 100 pieces, the signatures would have to be removed, and the buildings would no longer be recognizable. I would not go lower.

## 6. Production method

### 6.1 Format of the pieces: text

Each piece is a **character grid** in a `.js` or `.json` file. It can be edited in any editor, including on the phone, and versions can be compared with `diff`. Each character is a **semantic index**, not a color:

```
// shuttered window, 6×7, anchor at bottom center
'win_shutter': { w:6, h:7, anchor:[3,7], rows:[
  'oooooo',
  'oAaaAo',
  'oA..Ao',   // '.' transparent, 'o' outline
  'oA..Ao',   // 'A' light accent, 'a' dark accent
  'oAaaAo',
  'oooooo',
  ' w  w ' ]} // 'w' wall ledge ("wall" ramp)
```

The indices are grouped by ramp, with 1 to 4 tones per ramp (1 = the lightest):

| Indices | Ramp |
|---|---|
| `o` | outline |
| `1-4` | main ramp (wall, body) |
| `a-d` | accent |
| `s-u` | skin |
| `h-j` | hair |
| `m-p` | metal |
| `.` | transparent |

**Metadata**:
- anchor (the ground point);
- attachment points: `hand`, `head`, `roof`, `door`;
- ramps used;
- mirror allowed or not.

### 6.2 Palettes

- **One master palette of 32 colors, organized in 8 ramps of 4 tones**: wood, stone, terracotta, vegetation, skin, fabric A, fabric B, metal. The outline is the darkest tone of each ramp, which gives a selective outline rather than black everywhere.
- **A "skin"** (theme) is a small object that says, for an instance, which real ramp goes into each semantic ramp:

  ```
  { main: 'bois_clair', accent: 'tuile', metal: 'cuivre' }
  ```

  Recoloring an instance amounts to changing this object.
- Seasons, night and biome are **ramp substitution tables**, applied to the whole rendering.

### 6.3 The generator to write (JavaScript, no dependency, about 1,500 lines)

| Module | Role | Lines (estimate) |
|---|---|---|
| `palette.js` | palette, ramps, themes, season tables | 100 |
| `pieces.js` | decoding of text grids, mirror, 90° rotation | 100 |
| `iso.js` | box, roofs, cylinders, tiers, 2:1 projection with textures | 350 |
| `building.js` | families, tiers, density, seed, modules at anchors | 250 |
| `actor.js` | layer composition, animations, directions | 200 |
| `terrain.js` | ground, path, water, hills with noise | 200 |
| `particles.js` | smoke, dust, weather, sparks | 150 |
| `layout.js` | sectors, slots, `v(n)`, `w(n)`, depth sorting | 150 |

- **Rendering**: 2D canvas, `putImageData` to compose, then a cached **sheet (atlas)** per key `type|tier|seed|season`.
- **Display** at integer scale ×2 or ×3, with `imageSmoothingEnabled = false`.
- **Reading the state**: `window.evolve.global` at our own pace, about once per second (§ 8).

### 6.4 Work order

1. Palette, piece format, decoder and textured isometric box. This is the foundation; we validate it before any drawing.
2. The terrain by code: grass, path, seasons.
3. The housing family in 3 tiers, with the anti-clone rule.
4. Placement from a **fake `global`**: sliders for each counter.
5. One body, 3 jobs (unemployed, lumberjack, farmer), walk and strike.

### 6.6 Throwaway prototype (`work/proto-sprites/`)

I wrote a 300-line test in standard Python, with no installed package: `proto.py`. It produces `planche.png` and `saisons.png`, at ×4 scale. It generates in 2.6 s, on the phone:
- 15 cabins in 3 tiers with variants, and 3 2×1 buildings;
- a mirror versus regenerated box comparison;
- 3 density levels;
- 18 character images in 4 themes;
- a cabin in 3 seasons.

The drawn pieces used are 8 8×8 textures, 12 character layers and 2 tools.

**What is validated**:
- **The tiers read at first glance**:
  - P1: logs and thatch;
  - P2: planks, shingles, base and chimney;
  - P3: stone or brick, tiles, and taller house.

  The change of material and height is enough. The whole village "changes era".
- **The variants do not look like clones.** Roof ramp, material, ridge direction, chimney and door position are enough for 4 neighboring cabins of the same tier to all be different.
- **The generated boxes have consistent lighting** and a clean pixel-art rendering. The 8×8 textures tiled on the slanted faces work without touch-up.
- **The mirror breaks the light**: the check is done. The mirrored building has its long facade lit on the right, next to buildings lit on the left. The box regenerated in the other direction is right. Buildings must therefore be regenerated instead of mirrored.
- **Density by composition works**: annex (a 2nd small box), then upper floor (height and 2nd row of windows). There is no extra drawing.
- **Seasons by ramp swap** work: green, russet or white ground, and a snowy roof obtained by changing only the roof ramp.
- **Layered characters** give different citizens: skin, hair, outfit, hat, tool. The 3-pose leg walk and the strike with the tool rotated by 90° work.

**What the test taught (corrections to integrate)**:
1. **Small modules must be drawn already slanted.** A 4 px window drawn flat then slanted column by column gives a scribble. The door, wider (5 px), passes. Door and window must be drawn for the left face; the right face = mirror and one step darker, which the code does. This does not change the number of pieces.
2. **Modules must contrast with the wall.** A wooden door on a wooden wall disappears. A dark frame and a light leaf are needed, and a light metal accent (the handle).
3. **Ground noise must be in clumps** (3×2 patches and a few tufts), not pixel by pixel. Otherwise you get TV snow.
4. **Tile textures are too busy** with 3 tones. A maximum of 2 tones is needed on P3 roofs.
5. **At 16×24, the tool alone is not enough to recognize the job.** The axe is mostly visible with arms raised. What reads first is **the hat and the outfit color**. This confirms the order of the compensations in § 3.5: outfit, headgear, place, then exaggerated tool.

**Real observed cost**: for the box, the roof, the modules and the character, about 300 lines. This agrees with the estimate of 1,500 lines for the complete generator in JavaScript.

### 6.7 Quality criteria

| Criterion | Test |
|---|---|
| Silhouette | Fill the piece in black: the building or the job must remain recognizable. |
| Readability at ×1 | Display it at real size on the phone. If you hesitate, enlarge the tool or the signature, not the detail. |
| Contrast | At least 2 value steps between a building and the ground, and between a character and the ground or the wall behind. The test is done in grayscale. |
| Single light | Light from the top left. Left face light (tone 2), right face medium (tone 3), roof on the light side the lightest (tone 1), cast shadow at the bottom right. The generator enforces it on the boxes; the drawn pieces must follow. |
| Short ramps | 3 to 4 tones per ramp, no color outside the palette, no improvised anti-aliasing. |
| Anti-clone | No identical neighbor on the sheet of 24 instances. |

## 7. Decisions to settle (with my recommendation)

| Decision | Options | My recommendation |
|---|---|---|
| View | 2:1 isometric or 3/4 front view | **2:1 isometric**. It lends itself to generated boxes and grid placement; the 3/4 view would force drawing the facades. |
| Tile size | 16×8, 32×16 or 64×32 | **32×16**. 16×8 is too small for the signatures; 64×32 quadruples the area to draw. |
| Starting species | human, or the played species (`global.race.species`) | **Human first.** The other species are done by skin recoloring, plus a "species head" layer: about 3 pieces per genus, instead of the +170 images of report 8. |
| Cap of visible buildings | 40, 80 or 120 | **80 in total, 24 per type**, with the `v(n)` curve and the density. |
| Cap of visible citizens | 20, 40 or 80 | **40**, with `w(n)`. |
| Character directions | front only, or front and back | **Front and back** (+8 pieces). Front only gives people who walk "backwards" when they move away. |
| Outline | black, selective, or none | **Selective**, with the darkest tone of the ramp. |
| Night | none, or real time | **None at first.** Evolve has no time of day; we will add a filter later if we want. |
| Interface | in pixel art, or Evolve's HTML | **Evolve's HTML.** Only the village window goes to pixel art. |
| Finish | 100% generated, or touch-up of the 5 or 6 flagship buildings | **Touch-up of the flagship buildings**, for +10 to 15 pieces. |
| Palette | 32 free colors, or 8 ramps × 4 | **8 ramps × 4**, indispensable for recoloring. |

## 8. What is verified in the code, and what is my judgment

### Verified in Evolve's sources (read, not executed)

- **Reading the state**: `window.evolve.global` is a **deep copy** (`deepClone`) of `global`. It is redone by `updateDebugData()` at each pass of `fastLoop` (`src/main.js` around line 8097, `src/debug.js` lines 41 to 47), when `global.settings.expose` is true (default value `false`, `src/vars.js` line 1529). The main loop runs every ~250 ms × modifier (`src/functions.js` line 156). The copy is therefore fresh; reading it once per second is enough.
- **Buildings**: `global.city.<key>.count`. The 26 keys of report 8 exist in `src/actions.js`. Some also have an `on` field: `compost`, `mill`, `garrison`, `sawmill`, `cement_plant`, `mine`, `coal_mine`. The exact meaning of `on` is not confirmed (see the next list).
- **The mill changes name** when `tech.agriculture >= 5` ("windmill", next era). It is a natural tier for era 2.
- **Jobs**: `global.civic.<job>.workers` (`src/jobs.js`). Soldiers are in `global.civic.garrison.workers`, with also `wounded` (`src/civics.js`). The population is read in `global.resource[global.race.species].amount`.
- **Tech tiers** (`src/tech.js`, `grant` fields):

  | Key | Levels |
  |---|---|
  | `axe` | 1 stone_axe, 2 copper_axes, 3 iron_axes |
  | `saw` | 1 iron_saw |
  | `housing` | 1 housing, 2 cottage |
  | `agriculture` | 1 to 4 for era 1 |
  | `mining` | 1 mining, 2 metal_working, 3 iron_mining, 4 coal_mining |
  | `smelting` | 1, 2 steel |
  | `cement` | 1 |
  | `science` | 1 to 4 |
  | `currency` | 1 to 4 |
  | `primitive` | 1 to 3 |

- **Calendar** (`src/vars.js` line 1847, `src/seasons.js`, `src/main.js`): `global.city.calendar` contains:

  | Field | Values |
  |---|---|
  | `day`, `year` | counters |
  | `season` | 0 spring, 1 summer, 2 autumn, 3 winter |
  | `weather` | 0 precipitation, 1 cloudy, 2 sun |
  | `temp` | 0 cold (precipitation becomes snow), 1, 2 hot |
  | `wind` | 0 or 1 |
  | `moon` | 0 to 27 |

  The day advances in `longLoop`. **There is no time of day.**
- **Biome**: `global.city.biome`; 12 biomes in `src/races.js` (grassland, oceanic, forest, desert, volcanic, tundra, savanna, swamp, ashland, taiga, hellscape, eden). There are also planet traits in `global.city.ptrait`.

### Not verified

- **Not verified in the code**:
  - the exact meaning of `on` for each building (active, powered?);
  - that `resource.X.max` exists for all resources (for filling the piles);
  - the real unlock order in the game, since I did not play.
- **My designer's judgment, not measured**:
  - all piece sizes, pixel areas and piece counts;
  - the `v(n)` and `w(n)` curves and the caps;
  - the tier triggers (`axe >= 2`, `cement >= 1`), which are a staging choice and not a game rule;
  - the estimate of 1,500 lines of code;
  - the "stays beautiful" judgment.
- The "before" figures come from report 8, and the "before" areas are my calculation from its sizes.
- **The prototype** (`work/proto-sprites/`) validates **by eye**:
  - the textured boxes, the tiers and the variants;
  - the density and the seasons;
  - the layered characters.

  It does not validate the ×1 rendering on the phone screen, nor performance in JavaScript, nor the hookup to `window.evolve.global`. Neither the prototype nor Evolve has been executed in a browser.
